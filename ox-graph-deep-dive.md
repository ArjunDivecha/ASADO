## Part 0 — The bottom line

Ox asked *"how do we make the graph a signal source?"* and answered with fourteen ways to build more features. After grounding in the current ledgers and the governing agenda, that is the wrong first question. The graph program already **is** a signal source — the only multi-specification, PIT-confirmed mechanism family in the ledger — and its actual situation is:

- its component ICs ran **negative from ~2024-04 to mid-2026** (flip autopsy: state-independent, not crowding, not construction — INCONCLUSIVE, working hypothesis "absorption-speed compression");
- the family **re-armed 2026-08-20** (two consecutive positive month-ends, June+July 2026) — a *sizing* gate, not a research verdict;
- its measured capture on the tradable US-listed ETF at the US close is **approximately zero** (the expression gap — the binding constraint per Research-Agenda-2026-07-v2);
- and the features themselves are **one economic theme in six proxies** (trade-edge convergence, six transforms), a fact the S2 design doc already conceded.

So the right question is: **what should a graph program look like when its one proven edge just spent 2.5 years wrong-way, re-armed yesterday, and can't yet be captured at the venue we trade?** Three principles organize everything below:

1. **The sign is the research object, not a nuisance.** A negative IC on "neighbors outran me → I catch up" *is* a positive IC on the reverse trade. The 2024–26 flip is information about how linkage transmission works now, and this doc's flagship proposal (#15–16) is a design that discriminates *why* it flipped — including a composition hypothesis the autopsy never tested.
2. **Mechanism diversity over proxy multiplication.** Nothing new gets built in the return-diffusion channel unless it carries *different economics* (forced flows, solvency stress, competition, structural change) — not a seventh transform of the same trade matrix.
3. **Sign-agnostic value first.** Four of the graph's six possible roles (conditioning, risk structure, estimation structure, Layer-2 context) don't care which way diffusion points. They are the highest-certainty payoff while the sign question resolves, and today they are almost entirely unbuilt.

## Part III — The flagship: turn the sign flip into the research program (#15–16)

### #15 — The two-sided transmission test: convergence vs continuation, by horizon. **[Repo-grounded; design]**

Every existing feature is registered one-directionally: positive gap → catch-up. The post-2024 record *is* evidence for the opposite regime at 21–63d, while the fastest lead-lag leg (LL_LEADER_RET_1D) stayed positive (+0.025 post-2024) — and the autopsy's structure finding (slow horizons died first and worst; the adaptive combiner died last) says the transmission *speed* changed, not just the amount. Ox treats the graph as a feature factory; this treats it as an instrument for measuring **the market's current absorption speed of linkage information**.

Design (one registered family batch, charged to network_spillover, ~4–6 trials):
- Re-register the canonical gap signal at horizons {1d, 5d, 21d, 63d} as **two-sided** hypotheses (direction is currently free to the registrant and flips are never charged — ox #24; pre-registering both directions explicitly is the honest version).
- Report the **transmission-sign curve**: IC by horizon × sub-period (pre-2024 / 2024–26 / post-re-arm). Pre-2024 the curve should be positive everywhere; the 2024–26 regime should show positive-fast/negative-slow if absorption-speed compression is right — that *shape* is the diagnostic the INCONCLUSIVE autopsy lacked.
- Failure modes are informative in both directions: flat-zero everywhere = the edge is gone (G2 dies honestly); positive-fast/negative-slow = harvest at 1–5d expressions (the daily-horizon cost kill is retracted-class, untested); negative everywhere = the continuation/competition story (#16).

This is cheap (all inputs exist), serves G1's open diagnosis directly, and converts the re-arm from "hope it holds" into a measured object.

### #16 — The edge-composition hypothesis: complementarity vs competition edges. **[New mechanism; BACI build]**

The autopsy tested crowding, regime, and construction drift — and rejected all three. It never tested **edge composition**. The aggregate bilateral trade edge mixes two economically opposite relationships:

- **Complementary linkage** (vertical/customer-supplier trade): partner's good news is my good news → *positive* transmission. This is the mechanism behind country trade momentum (Rizova, JIFMIM 2013 — **[CONFIRMED from the full paper]**: trade-partner prior-month returns predict a country's next-month index return; five-factor alpha 1.21%/mo, t=4.54, in the headline equal-weighted spec, 0.83–1.21%/mo across all six specs, July 1981–March 2009, universe growing from ~10–12 to ~30 countries, max 46; the signal survives controls for own-country momentum **and** a global country-momentum factor **and** an FX-momentum factor — exactly the orthogonality G2's kill test demands; weaker for large/heavily-covered countries, consistent with gradual information diffusion) and the firm-level economic-links literature (its industry-level cousin, Shahrur-Becker-Rosenfeld FAJ 2010, is a distinct result — supplier-*industry* portfolios across 22 DMs, ~15%/yr — not to be conflated). **The decisive verification finding: no country-level replication or decay study of Rizova exists post-2009** (10 total citing works; none re-tests the strategy). Sixteen years of out-of-sample data are sitting in ASADO's own warehouse, untested by anyone. (Quantpedia prior-art check: the catalog's only "International Trade Momentum" entry, #0790, is Hong 2022 — a firm-level US-stocks construction, not Rizova's country strategy — and is UNSCREENED; no network/linkage theme anywhere in the mirror carries a DIES_BOTH kill.)
- **Competitive linkage** (export-basket overlap into the same third markets): partner's good news is my *lost market share* → *negative* transmission. Hidalgo-Hausmann product-space logic; no one has tested it as a transmission-sign moderator at country level.

If the composition of world trade shifted post-2018/2022 toward competition-dominant relationships (fragmentation, China re-routing through connector economies, friend-shoring), the *blended* edge's transmission sign degrades and can flip — **producing exactly the state-independent, no-crowding-fingerprint decay the autopsy found**, because the cause lives in the weights, not in the market state or in positioning.

Build: BACI (CEPII) gives bilateral trade at HS6 product level, ~200 countries, 1995–2024 (HS92 vintage; later HS vintages start later), open license (Etalab 2.0), direct bulk ZIP, latest release 202601 (Jan 30, 2026) **[CONFIRMED by fetching the CEPII page]**. From it, per country-pair-year, compute (a) a **complementarity weight** (trade intensity in vertically-related products) and (b) a **competition weight** (cosine similarity of the two countries' export baskets — a new `similar-as-competitor` edge family, structurally different from the fundamentals-similarity twins). Then split the existing gap features into complementary-weighted and competition-weighted variants and test the transmission sign on each, including whether the *aggregate* flip is explained by the mix shift (a decomposition, not just two more signals).

Verdict-relevant discipline: registered as network_spillover trials (same mechanism class — honest charging); the *decomposition finding* (does the mix shift explain the flip?) goes to the methodology ledger against G1. Publication lag: BACI's own release cadence is ~1 year behind the reference year — the edges are structural, slow, and PIT-safe with an explicit `applies_from` per the `collect_pit_edges.py` template.

Why this is the single highest-leverage new build: it is the only idea on the table that can *explain* the flip rather than route around it, it reuses the entire existing feature machinery, and its two sub-edges are each independently useful afterward (competition edges are a genuinely new similarity family; complementarity edges are the clean Rizova test G2 wants).

## Part IV — The new ideas, by role

Numbering continues from ox-graph-research.md (#1–14). Each idea: mechanism, clock, data status, where it charges, and what would kill it.

### R2 — Signal features (mechanism-diverse only)

**#17 — Flow-pressure channel: the JLR push-exposure score. [CONFIRMED from the primary paper]** The one predictive mechanism class the ledger has *never* tested: **forced flows through common holders** — economically distinct from information diffusion (it predicts pressure-then-reversal, not drift). Jotikasthira-Lundblad-Ramadorai (JF 2012) demonstrated the country-level channel with EPFR fund data (1,175 funds, 25 EMs, 1996–2009): their At-Risk measure — Σ over funds of (trailing fund-flow shock × lagged country allocation × fund TNA), normalized by country market cap — sorted weekly gives a top-minus-bottom quintile spread of **~176bp that reverses almost fully over ~12 weeks**, amplified when G-7 returns are in their tails. The authors state plainly that the tradable leg is the **reversal**, not the run-up. No academic follow-up turns this into a standing country signal — open ground. ASADO's version: monthly per-country **push exposure** = Σ over holder countries of (holder-country flow/stress state, fast: from existing foreign/ETF flow series + holder-country market stress) × (holder→country weight from the CPIS holder vintages, slow: annual +9mo). Register under a **new canonical family** (`flow_pressure` — genuinely different mechanism; requires the family_registry.yaml edit per I.2) with both a pressure leg and a reversal leg pre-registered. Kill: no IC at any horizon 5d–63d, or fully spanned by the existing ETF_FLOW contrarian signal. Related quick win: ox #1's Anton-Polk overlap edges are the *comovement* face of the same holder data — build both off one overlap-edge collector; and note the TIC monthly rows already in `bilateral_portfolio_matrix.parquet` (excluded from the PIT holder history, which keeps only `imf_pip`) offer a **monthly-clock US-holder edge** at near-zero cost. One boundary the verification drew: a full Greenwood-Landier-Thesmar fire-sale *vulnerability index* port to countries has never been published (well-triangulated absence; closest is a BoJ three-jurisdiction sectoral model), and the obstacle isn't the holdings matrix — it's that "leverage" and "asset illiquidity" have no validated country-level proxies. So #17 deliberately takes the JLR flow-pressure form (needs only flows × holder weights), not the GLT index form.

**#18 — Trade-imbalance centrality (CBC), the FX leg. [CONFIRMED — and now peer-reviewed: Journal of Financial Economics 172 (Oct 2025), art. 104112]** Hou–Sarno–Ye: centrality on the *imbalance* network (who runs deficits into whom — direction matters, sign matters) predicts **currency** excess returns. Exact construction verified (eq. 24): CBC = [wI + (1−w)V](I − αA₀′)⁻¹(A₀′ − A₀)ℓ — a Leontief/Katz-Bonacich propagation on the transposed imbalance matrix blended with the currency covariance matrix; calibrated w≈0.52, α≈0.68; H-L Sharpe **0.65 (static parameters) / 0.54 (yearly re-calibrated)**, up to 41 currencies, and it *spans* carry, dollar, Richmond's trade-network factor, and the intermediary factors rather than being spanned by them. Buildable today: the imbalance matrix is exports−imports from the same IMF IMTS pulls that feed `graph_edge_vintages` (weights currently keep only the sum; the collector needs a signed variant). Primary registration on the **FX leg** (serves the agenda's E3/FX-expression territory, where the venue problem is smallest); a secondary equity-index trial only after the FX result. Distinct economics from the gap features (external-balance positioning, not return diffusion) — candidate new family `external_balance`. Kill: spanned by carry/dollar factors ASADO already has, or IMTS imbalance data too noisy at 34 countries.

**#19 — Sudden-stop partner exposure.** Forbes-Warnock episode logic on data in hand: flag each country whose **largest trade/banking partners are currently in a gross-inflow stop episode** (episodes from existing flow series; partner weights from existing vintages). A conditioning-first feature: interact it with the gap features (per the Hale-Kapan-Minoiu finding that network transmission is crisis-conditional) rather than registering it standalone first. Context tier → one registered interaction trial if the conditional IC separates.

### R4 — Regime / conditioning columns (sign-agnostic; mostly context tier, few trials)

**#20 — The exposure-graph state package (extends ox #9–10).** Ox's five scalars (density, path length, clustering, fragmentation, cross-layer spread) plus two verified additions: (a) **algebraic connectivity / percolation threshold of the CPIS holder graph** — the one structural indicator in the whole search with a documented multi-year lead (fell toward zero in 2005, three years pre-GFC; arXiv 1306.0215) [UNVERIFIED at panel level — treat as lead, not law]; (b) **3-year growth of inbound cross-border bank claims / GDP** — the BIS EWI with real out-of-sample discipline (AUC ~0.6–0.65 at 3y horizon, BIS QR March 2018), computable by summing the existing BIS edge vintages. All context tier: they gate sizing and detector thresholds, never trade. The density-direction trap (ox #10) governs: exposure-graph density and market-estimated spillover density move oppositely in crises — never one heuristic for both.

**#21 — Return-topology columns (the fast complement, honestly labeled).** From daily returns only: normalized MST/TMFG tree length, absorption ratio (top-eigenvalue variance share), spectral-gap derivative. The literature verdict is blunt: these are **descriptive/coincident, not predictive** — the strongest TDA early-warning claim collapses to a ~46% hit rate under honest testing, and Ricci curvature was demoted by its own follow-up literature to an ex-post crash hallmark [REFUTED as predictor — do not register as alpha]. Land them as brief/regime context (market-compression state for the combiner to condition on) and as inputs to #22's divergence detector. Cheap (an afternoon), useful, never a signal claim.

**#22 — The divergence detector (upgrade of ox #10's trap into a detector).** The one topology signal with a mechanism: **exposure graph freezing/fragmenting while the return-correlation graph tightens** (deleveraging + market synchronization) is the classic crisis fingerprint. Nightly scalar: divergence between #20's exposure-state and #21's return-topology state; fires as a D-series detector row (loud DETECTOR_DEGRADED discipline, D10 as the code template per `build_dislocations.py:909-919`).

**#23 — Country-DebtRank as a stress column.** DebtRank-class nonlinear, buffer-normalized propagation on the BIS quarterly vintages, with IMF FSI regulatory-capital ratios (public, annual; FX reserves fallback) as the buffer proxy — a few hundred lines. The honest literature read: methodologically mature, **no published out-of-sample test that DebtRank beats linear centrality for prediction** — that horse race doesn't exist; ASADO would be running it. So: context tier first (a per-country stress score + a network-wide amplifying/dissipating regime flag from the Bardoscia phase-transition result), and exactly **one** registered trial (DebtRank score vs the existing Katz feature, does nonlinearity add anything) before any further investment. Kill: indistinguishable from linear centrality — likely, and worth knowing cheaply.

### R5 — Estimation structure (sign-agnostic; methodology ledger, not signal trials)

**#24 — Graph-structured cross-sectional estimation (the NVAR template). [CORRECTED by verification — read carefully]** Mlikota's Network-VAR (arXiv 2211.13610, v7 Jan 2026, unpublished) cut one-step OOS macro-forecast MSE 13–68% vs an *oracle-tuned* dynamic factor model on 34–42 countries — but the verification pass found the widely-repeated framing is wrong: in that country application **the network is fully estimated from the data (ridge/lasso shrunk toward zero), not anchored to observed trade links** (an observed input-output network is used only in his separate US-sector application), and one configuration (lasso/MPM on GDP) actually *loses* to the DFM. So the honest import is narrower and still valuable: **sparse network-structured cross-sectional dynamics beat factor models at exactly ASADO's N** — the case for anchoring to the *observed* trade matrix is a hypothesis to test (estimated-W vs trade-W vs zero-W as a three-way comparison), not settled science. Port the *estimator shape*: (a) a Laplacian penalty pulling per-country signal weights toward trade/bank neighbors (Li-Li network-constrained regression — genomics-validated, finance-untested); (b) an NVAR-style spillover fit as the *estimated* replacement for the heuristic neighbor-gap — also the disciplined version of the SAR white space (no one in the spatial-econometrics literature uses SAR fitted values or residuals as trading signals — open ground, but run the N=34 Monte Carlo on estimator/normalization first). Lands in the ML plan as an **M3/M4-adjacent baseline enhancement** (linear, auditable, walk-forward) — it must not skip the milestone ladder; methodology-ledger entry, not a family trial, until it produces a concrete signal candidate.

**#25 — Graph-target covariance shrinkage.** Ledoit-Wolf with the shrinkage *target* built from graph-implied correlation (trade/bank edge intensity) instead of constant-correlation. No literature precedent found either way — original, cheap, sign-agnostic, and it pays even if diffusion alpha never comes back (better 34×34 covariance = better paper-book construction and better severity normalization everywhere). One methodology experiment: realized OOS portfolio risk match vs standard targets.

### R6 — Layer-2 context (no constitution conflict, no trials — pure leverage)

**#26 — Ego-network evidence packs.** Evidence packs today carry **zero neighbor context** (`build_evidence_packs.py` — no adjacency join at all). Restructure each pack as a small subgraph walk: the dislocation country + its top-k trade/bank/holder neighbors' current states (returns, active dislocations, flow states) + recent edge deltas + the historical analog set. The verified basis is modest but real: graph-structured retrieval measurably helps multi-hop/multi-entity LLM reasoning (HippoRAG-2-class results), with one hard guardrail — GraphRAG win-rates are inflated by LLM-judge artifacts, so if we A/B it, use blinded, position-randomized scoring, or don't bother measuring. This is the cheapest idea in the doc relative to its leverage: the Layer-2 pass (3 theses in 10 weeks; ox's meta-finding) is starved of exactly the relational context the graph already holds.

**#27 — LLM-extracted event edges: calibration protocol, not a feed (extends ox #4).** New verified number: LLM relation-extraction precision runs ~60–65% even with reflection passes (FinReflectKG-EvalBench).
